\paragraph{Representations.} When the public scGPT-spatial checkpoint is loaded on GPUs without FlashAttention, the
attention weights are silently not loaded because parameter names are not remapped; we remapped them and verified
every tensor. Embeddings were extracted in half precision, and for assays where more than 5\% of cells exceed the
1{,}200-gene input (where genes are sampled at random) we averaged five extraction seeds.

\paragraph{Hyperparameters.}\label{sec:supp_hp} $\alpha=0.3$, $\gamma=0.4$ ($\gamma=1$ at the first iteration), $\varepsilon=0.1$, $k=30$,
sketches of 4{,}300 cells, 10 EM iterations of 100 Adam steps (learning rate $10^{-3}$), $\lambda_{\mathrm{fit}}=0.9$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{id}}=0.3$, reference subset of 20{,}000 cells with a per-class floor of 200, drawn without replacement with a fixed random seed so that the subset is the same for every run,
512 landmarks with $\beta=2$ (USHER-ST): with $D_{il}$ the hop distance from cell $i$ to landmark $l$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:landmark}
  C^{s}_{ik}=-\beta^{-1}\log\textstyle\sum_{l=1}^{L}\exp\bigl(-\beta(D_{il}+D_{kl})\bigr),\qquad L=512,\ \beta=2.
\end{equation} Entropic filtering removes rows above the 90th percentile of entropy or below
the 10th percentile of row maximum.

\subsection*{S2. Metric definitions}\label{sec:supp_metrics}
Group scores follow \texttt{scib-metrics} without min--max scaling across methods: batch\_v2 is the mean of iLISI,
kBET, BRAS and graph connectivity; bio\_v2 is the mean of NMI, ARI, label ASW, cLISI and isolated-label score;
total\_v2 $=0.4\,$batch\_v2 $+\,0.6\,$bio\_v2. xfer\_main is the balanced accuracy of a 15-NN vote of reference
labels, after centring on the reference mean and unit normalisation, over shared recoverable groups; xfer\_all is the
same over all groups with at least 20 cells on both sides. rare\_purity is the fraction of same-label reference
neighbours among the 15-NN of cells from groups below 2\% of held-out tiles. knn\_xen is the balanced accuracy of
predicting a cell's microenvironment class (eight $k$-means classes of spatial-neighbour label composition) from its 15
reference neighbours. In the GraphST comparison, mixing is $1-\max(f_{\mathrm{same}}-0.5,0)/0.5$, with
$f_{\mathrm{same}}$ the fraction of same-platform cells among 15 neighbours in the pooled unit-normalised embedding;
transfer uses no centring.

\subsection*{S4. Microenvironment term}\label{sec:supp_niche}
\paragraph{Construction.} For each cell, the raw counts of its 15 nearest spatial neighbours (Euclidean distance
between cell positions within the same assay, the cell itself excluded) are summed over the genes shared by the pair.
The summed vectors of reference and source are pooled, scaled to $10^{4}$ counts and log-transformed, restricted to
2{,}000 highly variable genes, standardised per gene and reduced to 50 principal components, giving $\mathbf{N}_i$.
The neighbourhood has a fixed number of cells, not a fixed radius, and no correction for local cell density is
applied: the count scaling removes the total count of the neighbourhood but not its spatial extent, which varies with
density. The structure matrices are built on $[\mathbf{x}_i,\,s\mathbf{N}_i]$ with
$s=\bigl(\tfrac{1}{n_t}\sum_j\|\mathbf{x}^{t}_j\|_2\bigr)\big/\bigl(\tfrac{1}{n_t}\sum_j\|\mathbf{N}^{t}_j\|_2\bigr)$,
computed on the reference, so that the two blocks have the same scale; otherwise the larger block would dominate the
$k$-NN graph. Before the graph is built, rows are $\ell_2$-normalised and centred and, only when their dimension
exceeds 64, projected onto the 64 leading eigenvectors of their covariance, as in the USHER implementation. On PCA
pairs $[\mathbf{x},s\mathbf{N}]$ has 100 dimensions and is projected, whereas the 50-dimensional embedding used by
USHER-CC is not; this is the fourth difference listed below.

\paragraph{Spatial extent.} The median 15th-neighbour distance of Stereo-seq (25.0--29.5\um) lies inside the range of
the other platforms (16.7--33.9\um), and the median diameter of its 4-cell pseudo-spots (58.8\um in COAD, 65.4\um in
OV) is exceeded by CosMx 6K in COAD (59.4\um), so neither measure singles out the platform on which the
microenvironment term has an effect.

\paragraph{Confounds.} In the three-arm comparison, adding the microenvironment (arm (b) to (c)) leaves convergence
unchanged on average (1.08-fold) but makes it 2.44-fold slower on COAD Stereo-seq. Across the 12 pairs, \frost and USHER-CC give batch\_v2 $p=0.52$, bio\_v2 $p=0.95$,
total\_v2 $p=0.43$, xfer\_main $p=0.85$, xfer\_all $p=0.26$, rare\_purity $p=0.72$, knn\_xen $p=0.30$ and
imputation $p=0.23$ (exact Wilcoxon, $n=12$; largest absolute median difference 0.0008). The difference between \frost and USHER-CC measures the joint effect of three simultaneous changes to $\Cs$: adding
the microenvironment, replacing landmark distances with exact geodesics, and replacing per-iteration recomputation on
$f_\theta(\mathbf{X}^{s})$ with a single computation on the unmapped source. On the PCA pairs a fourth difference is
added: the 64-dimensional low-rank projection applies to the augmented features but not to the 50-dimensional
embeddings. Three diagnostic arms (Table~\ref{M-tab:arms}) separate these changes partly: arm (b) differs from arm (a) in the
last two, and arm (c) differs from arm (b) only in the microenvironment on the two scGPT-spatial pairs, which include
Stereo-seq; on the two PCA pairs it also changes the projection. The 15-neighbour radius that defines the microenvironment differs between platforms (16.7--33.9\um). A microenvironment-only variant (structure on $\mathbf{N}$ alone) was run with a single seed
and is reported only in Table~\ref{tab:imp_rev}. Under the reversed
imputation protocol it recovers part of the gene-level loss on COAD Stereo-seq (Table~\ref{tab:imp_rev}), still
below no alignment.

\subsection*{S7. Future directions}\label{sec:supp_future}
\paragraph{Unbalanced or partial transport.} \frost, like USHER, solves balanced FGW, so all source mass is
transported, although sections from different platforms need not cover the same tissue and the source may contain
cell types absent from the reference. Formulations that relax the marginals so that unmatched mass stays
untransported, as RAFT-UP~\citep{wu2026raftup} does for partially overlapping slices, address both cases directly;
we have not evaluated them. If such solvers are slower, approximate entropic transport such as LCN-Sinkhorn~\citep{gasteiger2021lcn}
or Geodesic Sinkhorn~\citep{huguet2022geodesic} could offset the cost.

\paragraph{Feature-level and feature-weighted FGW.} \frost couples cells only, and its global map blurs gene-level
neighbourhoods (main text, Section~\ref{M-sec:limitations}). CO-Optimal Transport (COOT)~\citep{redko2020coot} optimises two transport
maps simultaneously, one between samples and one between features; a coupling at the feature level is a named route
to that limitation. The structure of \frost is built on $[\mathbf{x},s\mathbf{N}]$ with $s$ set once, by hand, from
the ratio of mean $\ell_2$ norms. fsFGW~\citep{lee2026fsfgw} adds adaptive feature-compression weights to the FGW
objective because existing formulations treat all features uniformly, which is a principled replacement for that
fixed weight. Linear FGW~\citep{nguyen2022linearfgw} embeds graphs in a vector space so that kernel matrices over
many graphs are computed faster than with pairwise FGW; \frost computes a single coupling between two sets, so that
speed-up does not apply here.

\paragraph{Global map with local correction.} Under the leakage-controlled protocol on COAD Stereo-seq, the local
correction MNN-ref reaches 0.2015, above no alignment (0.1850), whereas \frost reaches 0.1251 (Table~\ref{tab:imp_rev}).
A hybrid that applies the global linear map and then a light local correction is therefore worth testing.

\paragraph{Other settings.} The per-class floor was varied on the COAD pairs only (Table~\ref{tab:sens}); a floor
set from the data is an alternative. Replacing CORAL by whitening with diagonal shrinkage of the covariances has not
been measured.
