\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{GraphST fails in the reference-preserving setting}\label{sec:res_spatial}
On a common subsample of 10{,}000 cells per side (Table~\ref{tab:graphst}), \frost transfers labels more accurately
than GraphST (median paired difference $+0.377$, $p=0.0005$; $n=12$ throughout, GraphST averaged over three seeds),
with no significant difference in mixing ($+0.092$, $p=0.064$). GraphST is also worse than no alignment on transfer
(mean $-0.243$, $p=0.0015$) and better only in mixing (mean $+0.377$, $p=0.0005$), the signature of
over-correction; \frost improves on no alignment in both ($p=0.0010$ and $0.0005$).

The smallest \frost$-$GraphST gap in label transfer across the twelve pairs (0.211) exceeds the largest across-seed
standard deviation observed for GraphST (0.019) by more than an order of magnitude; seed choice does not account for
the gap (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:graphst_seeds}).
GraphST also moves the reference (drift 0.965--0.989 across pairs and seeds, mean 0.979), so anything built on it
must be rebuilt; this follows from re-embedding both sections and so holds for any joint integration method. A secondary sweep of GraphST's
\texttt{n\_neighbors} (default 3; also 6 and 12) on the four COAD pairs changed neither: transfer rose from 3 to 12 while
mixing fell monotonically, the smallest transfer gap to \frost was 0.239 and drift stayed at or above 0.939. On Visium
HD FF, GraphST mixes more than \frost at 3 and 6 neighbours (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:graphst_nn}).
GraphST had to be subsampled (its dense spatial
graph exhausted memory at 90{,}000 cells), whereas \frost runs on all 40{,}000--45{,}000 cells per side. GraphST
training took 883--1{,}152\,s per run on the subsample (mean 1{,}067\,s over 36 runs), and the \frost EM loop 387.3\,s
on all cells of the four COAD pairs at $T=25$ ($T=10$, used in the benchmark, was not timed), both excluding input preparation.

\subsection{Benchmark}\label{sec:res_bench}
Table~\ref{tab:main} gives the compact metrics (all 15 in Supplementary Figure~\ref{S-fig:grid}). On all seven
cell-level metrics \frost ranks among the top three of 11 methods, counting its two internal variants
(Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:main_variants}): first on
xfer\_all; second on batch\_v2, total\_v2, rare\_purity and knn\_xen; third on bio\_v2 and xfer\_main. The only column led by an external baseline is bio\_v2, led by ridge, which uses the same
reference-derived labels as \frost; the difference is not significant ($p=0.06$). The imputation column
uses the original protocol, in which the source embedding encodes the predicted gene; under a reversed protocol the
ranking reverses (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). UMAPs of the hardest pair, COAD Stereo-seq, and of every other pair are in
Supplementary Section~S6.

Ridge is the informative comparison (Supplementary Figure~\ref{S-fig:gaps}): it uses the same auxiliary labels and
moment matching as \frost and differs mainly in how cells are paired. With $n=12$ unless stated, \frost exceeds ridge
on batch\_v2 and total\_v2 (both $p=0.0005$) and knn\_xen ($p=0.0015$), and does not differ significantly on bio\_v2
(median $-0.006$, $p=0.06$), xfer\_main ($p=0.29$), xfer\_all ($n=11$, $p=0.64$) or rare\_purity ($p=0.68$). total\_v2 is not independent evidence: batch\_v2 spans 0.34--0.66 across methods
and bio\_v2 only 0.60--0.69, so total\_v2 follows mixing. The knn\_xen advantage is specific to ridge; against
centring and Harmony it is not significant. Read as a Pareto trade-off (Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}), \frost gains mixing over ridge
with no significant loss in conservation or transfer. The same
reading holds within the scGPT-spatial pairs ($n=7$, $p\le0.031$ for the four differences) and the PCA pairs ($n=5$,
same signs at the smallest attainable $p=0.0625$; Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:strat}). \frost exceeds Symphony, the published reference-mapping method, on all
seven metrics ($n=12$, $p$ from 0.0005 to 0.034); against centring and Harmony it is better on most metrics (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:center_harmony}), and
against Harmony, fitted transductively on the evaluation tiles, these margins are lower bounds (Section~\ref{sec:reps}).

\subsection{Comparison with USHER}\label{sec:res_usher}
On the four COAD pairs, with one USHER seed, no paired test can reach significance (floor $p=0.125$), so we report
differences descriptively (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:usher}); the pipelines differ in four components, and
\frost runs 10 iterations against USHER's 25. Total scores are similar (0.7055 for USHER, 0.7025
for \frost); USHER is numerically higher on batch mixing, \frost on conservation and on every task metric, most on
xfer\_all, rare\_purity and knn\_xen ($+0.034$ to $+0.038$), with 8 instead of 200 structure computations per pair and
about 7.5-fold less EM time (387.3 against 2{,}911\,s).

\section{Ablation studies}\label{sec:res_ablation}
\paragraph{Post-hoc moment matching.}\label{sec:res_coral}
Comparing USHER-CC with USHER-ST isolates CORAL. It raises batch\_v2 ($+0.127$, $p=0.0010$), total\_v2 ($+0.053$,
$p=0.0010$), rare\_purity ($+0.029$, $p=0.0015$) and xfer\_all ($+0.025$, $p=0.012$), with no significant change
in bio\_v2 ($p=0.23$), xfer\_main ($p=0.73$) or knn\_xen ($p=0.15$; $n=12$ throughout). CORAL is therefore justified at the cell level;
its cost appears at the gene level (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). On the four COAD pairs (three seeds) the
per-class floor of 200 raises rare\_purity by 0.020, about nine times the seed standard deviation, while every other
compact metric changes by at most 0.006.

\paragraph{Identity regularisation and M-step.}
Raising $\lambda_{\mathrm{id}}$ above 0.3 lowers mixing and total score; $\lambda_{\mathrm{id}}=0$ was evaluated
only with a two-layer M-step, which mixes more while the linear map transfers labels and predicts microenvironment
better. The two branches differ in architecture, $\lambda_{\mathrm{id}}$ and floor at once, so this is a
pipeline-level comparison; we keep the linear map (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:sens}).

\paragraph{Building structure once: bundled evidence on 12 pairs.}\label{sec:res_once}
USHER-CC rebuilds $\Cs$ on $f_\theta(\mathbf{X}^{s})$ at every iteration; \frost builds it once on the unmapped source
and caches it. Across the 12 pairs the two do not differ significantly on any metric ($n=12$, all $p\ge0.23$; largest absolute
median difference 0.0008; per-metric values in Supplementary Section~S4). This is bundled evidence, not a
single-variable ablation: the two configurations also differ in the landmark approximation, the microenvironment
block and, on the PCA pairs, the 64-dimensional projection (Supplementary Section~S4), so effects of opposite sign could cancel. A
comparison that isolates how $\Cs$ is built exists only on the four COAD pairs, below.

\paragraph{Structure matrices and microenvironment.}\label{sec:res_niche}
Three arms on the four COAD pairs (seed 0, 25 iterations; not directly comparable with the main benchmark) differ
only in how $\Cs$ is built (Table~\ref{tab:arms}): (a) landmarks recomputed every iteration, as in USHER-ST; (b) exact
geodesics, cached; (c) \frost, which adds the microenvironment. Going from (a) to (b) changes the approximation and the
recomputation together and leaves quality unchanged (total\_v2 $-0.005$, imputation $+0.001$), while the fraction of
assignments still changing falls from 0.046 to 0.008. Going from (b) to (c) adds only the microenvironment on the two
scGPT-spatial pairs; on the two PCA pairs it also changes the dimensionality projection. Stereo-seq is one of the
clean pairs, and it is where the effect appears: under the original imputation protocol, (c) raises Stereo-seq
imputation from 0.048 to 0.113 (bootstrap intervals 0.045--0.052 and 0.107--0.118), above no alignment (0.0945). Under the reversed
protocol the gain persists in the benchmark configuration (\frost $-$ USHER-CC $=+0.044$ on COAD Stereo-seq,
disjoint intervals), but \frost stays below no alignment (0.1251 against 0.1850; Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
Averaged over the 12 pairs the term is neutral, so we do not claim that it improves the method. We keep it because it
is the only component of \frost that uses spatial coordinates, it has no measurable cost, and it is the only one that
narrows the gene-level gap on Stereo-seq.

Stopping at 10 iterations costs a small, measurable amount: all three arms still change 6--8\% of assignments per iteration at iteration
10 (Supplementary Figure~\ref{S-fig:converge}), yet \frost scores total\_v2 0.7025 at 10 iterations (three seeds) and
0.706 at 25 (one seed) on the same pairs, against a per-pair seed standard deviation of about 0.002. A comparison of
arm (a) with USHER's convergence is in Supplementary Figure~\ref{S-fig:converge}.

Caching, not the landmark approximation, is also where the cost saving comes from. From arm (a) to arm (b), EM
wall-clock falls from 457.7\,s to 386.1\,s ($T=25$) although an exact build takes more thread time than a landmark
build (30.6 against 3.3\,s): the saving comes from building 4 rather than 100 source matrices. From (b) to (c), the
microenvironment block changes wall-clock by $+1.2$\,s, small against the spread across pairs (355--435\,s);
an EM iteration takes 15.4\,s with cached exact $\Cs$ and 18.3\,s with recomputed landmarks (Supplementary
Table~\ref{S-tab:cost}). Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:changes} maps every change from USHER to its ablation. Structure builds fall from $B(T+1)$ to $2B$, which no
longer depends on $T$: 5.5-fold fewer at $T=10$ and 13-fold fewer at $T=25$; wall-clock at $T=10$ was not measured.
