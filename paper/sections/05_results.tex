\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{A spatially aware baseline fails in the reference-preserving setting}\label{sec:res_spatial}
Table~\ref{tab:graphst} compares \frost with GraphST on a common subsample of 10{,}000 source and 10{,}000
reference cells per pair. \frost transfers labels more accurately than GraphST in all 12 pairs (mean transfer
0.7223 vs 0.3731; mean paired difference $+0.349$, range $+0.210$ to $+0.458$; exact Wilcoxon
$p=4.9\times10^{-4}$, the smallest value attainable with 12 pairs). It also mixes the platforms better in 10 of
12 pairs, so GraphST's deficit in transfer is not the price of better mixing.

GraphST is also worse than performing no alignment at all. Its transfer is below that of the unaligned embeddings
in 11 of 12 pairs (mean difference $-0.243$, $p=0.0015$). Its only gain over no alignment is in mixing
($+0.377$, 12 of 12 pairs), which is the signature of over-correction: the two platforms become indistinguishable
because cell-type structure has been destroyed.

The reference itself moves. GraphST re-embeds both sections, and the 15-NN neighbourhoods of reference cells
overlap little with those before integration (drift 0.966--0.989 across pairs, mean 0.979). Any classifier,
annotation or index built on the reference would have to be rebuilt. For \frost and every other reference-preserving
method, drift is exactly zero. This is a property of the architecture, not of tuning.

Scale differs as well. GraphST had to be run on 10{,}000 cells per side because its dense spatial adjacency
exhausted memory at 90{,}000 cells, whereas \frost runs on the full 40{,}000--45{,}000 cells per side.
GraphST was run once, at its published defaults; Section~\ref{sec:limitations} discusses what this comparison does
and does not establish. Wall-clock time for GraphST was not extracted~\todo{GraphST wall-clock from log 42}.

\subsection{Benchmark against reference-preserving and reference-modifying baselines}\label{sec:res_bench}
Figure~\ref{fig:grid} shows all 16 metrics per source technology (per tissue in Supplementary
Figure~\ref{fig:grid_tissue}). Because batch\_v2 and bio\_v2 are means of the metrics beside them, ranking all 16
would count the same information several times. We therefore summarise with a compact set (Table~\ref{tab:main}):
the two group scores, their weighted total, and the four task metrics. On all seven, \frost ranks among the top
three of 11 methods. It is first on xfer\_all; second on batch\_v2, total\_v2, rare\_purity and knn\_xen; third on
bio\_v2 and xfer\_main. Where \frost is not first, the leader is usually one of its own ablations. USHER-CC leads
batch\_v2, total\_v2 and rare\_purity; USHER-ST, which has no CORAL step, leads xfer\_main and knn\_xen. The one
baseline that leads a column is ridge on bio\_v2. Table~\ref{tab:main} also reports gene imputation under the original
protocol, where \frost ranks second. That protocol leaks the predicted gene into the source embedding, and when the
leak is removed the ranking reverses (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}); we draw no conclusion from that column.

Figure~\ref{fig:gaps} gives the paired tests against every baseline. Ridge is the informative comparison, because it
uses the same auxiliary labels and the same moment matching as \frost and differs mainly in how source cells are
paired with reference cells. \frost exceeds ridge in all 12 pairs on batch\_v2 (0.6593 vs 0.5447). It also exceeds
ridge in all 12 on total\_v2 (0.6724 vs 0.6303), but this is not independent evidence: batch\_v2 ranges from 0.34 to
0.66 across methods and bio\_v2 only from 0.60 to 0.69, so differences in total\_v2 are driven by mixing.
On the remaining metrics \frost and ridge are not distinguishable. Ridge is ahead on bio\_v2 in 9 of 12 pairs
(0.6874 vs 0.6811; median difference $-0.006$, $p=0.06$). \frost is ahead on xfer\_main in 7 of 12 (0.6891 vs
0.6867, $p=0.29$). \frost has the highest mean on xfer\_all, but it is ahead of ridge in only 5 of 12 pairs
($p=0.64$). The comparison is therefore best read as a Pareto trade-off rather than through a composite score:
relative to ridge, \frost gains in mixing at no statistically detectable cost in conservation or transfer.

Two simpler baselines also deserve mention. Per-assay mean centring is not significantly worse than \frost on
xfer\_main (median $+0.020$, $p=0.27$) or knn\_xen ($p=0.79$). \frost's advantage over centring lies in mixing,
conservation, xfer\_all and rare-type purity. Against Symphony, the published reference-mapping method, \frost is
ahead in all 12 pairs on batch\_v2, bio\_v2, xfer\_main and xfer\_all. Part of that gap may reflect Symphony's own
count-based representation (Section~\ref{sec:baselines}).

\subsection{Ablations}\label{sec:res_ablation}

\paragraph{Post-hoc moment matching.}\label{sec:res_coral}
Adding CORAL to USHER-ST (giving USHER-CC) raises mixing on the common subsample by $+0.191$ ($p=0.0015$) but
transfer by only $+0.0086$ (Table~\ref{tab:graphst}). In the main benchmark it raises batch\_v2 from 0.5479 to
0.6600, while xfer\_main is unchanged (0.6913 vs 0.6896). The step is therefore mainly a mixing correction. Its cost
in local density fidelity is reported in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\paragraph{Reference floor.}\label{sec:res_floor}
The per-class floor was fixed before evaluation, on a validation split of the training tiles. The rule was the
largest rare-type purity among floors that lost no more than 0.02 in xfer\_main or total score, with the 0.02
threshold set in advance. That rule chose 200. On held-out tiles, larger floors improve xfer\_main, xfer\_all and
rare-type purity slightly (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:floor}), so 200 is conservative rather than tuned on
evaluation data.

\paragraph{Computational cost of the structure matrices.}\label{sec:res_cost}
Table~\ref{tab:cost} times three structure configurations on the four COAD pairs (seed 0, 25 EM iterations): the
adaptive landmark $\Cs$ of USHER-ST, a frozen exact $\Cs$ on the embeddings alone, and \frost's frozen exact $\Cs$
on augmented features. Freezing $\Cs$ cuts the number of $\Cs$ builds from 100 to 4, a 25-fold reduction equal
to the number of EM iterations. Each exact build is more expensive than a landmark one, but total $\Cs$ time still falls 2.7-fold,
and EM wall-clock time falls from 457.7\,s to 386.1\,s per pair. Adding the microenvironment block costs
nothing measurable (387.3\,s, with the same 8 builds). These diagnostic runs use 25 iterations, whereas the
benchmark maps use 10. The number of builds for the frozen configurations does not depend on the number of
iterations.

\paragraph{Structure matrices and the microenvironment term.}\label{sec:res_niche}
The difference $\Delta=$ \frost $-$ USHER-CC measures the joint effect of three simultaneous changes to $\Cs$: adding
the microenvironment, replacing landmark distances with exact geodesics, and replacing per-iteration recomputation
on $f_\theta(\mathbf{X}^{s})$ with a single computation on the unmapped source. On the PCA pairs a fourth difference
is added: the 64-dimensional low-rank projection applies to the augmented features but not to the 50-dimensional
embeddings. We cannot separate the contribution of the microenvironment with the data at hand.

On the main benchmark $\Delta$ is indistinguishable from zero. Across the 17 metrics it is positive in 8 and
negative in 9, and exceeds the seed standard deviation in 6. On the five aggregates the mean difference lies between
$-0.0007$ and $+0.0003$, with 5--8 of 12 pairs positive. Two metrics reach $p<0.05$ (label ASW $+0.0005$, cLISI
$+0.0003$), with differences in the fourth decimal place. On the common subsample $\Delta$ is $+0.0009$ for transfer
and $+0.0011$ for mixing (both $p=0.53$). We therefore do not claim that the microenvironment improves cell-level
integration. We keep it in \frost because it is free and because of the one setting in which it changes a gene-level
outcome.

That setting is gene-expression imputation from the coarsest-sampled source. Under a leakage-controlled protocol
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}), $\Delta$ is $+0.0442$ for COAD Stereo-seq, with disjoint bootstrap confidence
intervals. It is $+0.0019$ and $-0.0062$ for HCC and OV Stereo-seq. For the nine non-Stereo-seq pairs only the
original protocol was archived, and there $\Delta$ averages $+0.0003$. Even for COAD Stereo-seq, no alignment does better (0.1850, against 0.1409 for the best
USHER-family variant). The microenvironment thus repairs part of the gene-level damage that alignment itself causes,
in one pair; it is not a source of spatial information. The microenvironment-only variant is larger still on all
three Stereo-seq pairs, but it is $0.0301$ below the mixed form on HCC Visium HD FFPE (disjoint intervals), and it
was run with one seed. The mixed form in
\frost keeps most of the Stereo-seq effect without that failure.

\subsection{Comparison with USHER}\label{sec:res_usher}
Table~\ref{tab:usher} compares \frost with the unmodified USHER reference implementation on the four COAD pairs. The
two pipelines differ in four components at once (Table~\ref{tab:usher_vs_frost}), so this is a pipeline-level
comparison, not an ablation. USHER was run with one seed, so differences are read through their consistency across
the four pairs rather than through a test (the smallest attainable $p$ with four pairs is 0.125).

The two methods sit at different points of the same trade-off, and their total scores are level (total\_v2 0.7055 vs
0.7025; \frost ahead in 2 of 4 pairs). USHER mixes the platforms better on four of five mixing metrics: iLISI $-0.029$,
kBET $-0.071$, BRAS $-0.015$, batch ASW $-0.012$ and batch\_v2 $-0.028$ (\frost minus USHER); graph connectivity
is level. \frost conserves biology better on the cluster-based metrics, NMI $+0.037$ and ARI $+0.039$ (4 of 4 pairs
each), and on cLISI $+0.010$ (3 of 4) and bio\_v2 $+0.014$ (4 of 4). It is slightly lower on label ASW ($-0.010$)
and isolated labels ($-0.007$). It is also ahead on every task metric. The gains are small on xfer\_main ($+0.004$, 3 of 4) and clear on
the three metrics that probe rare types and local geometry: xfer\_all $+0.038$, rare-type purity $+0.038$ and
knn\_xen $+0.034$, each in 4 of 4 pairs. These are the metrics outside USHER's own benchmark. Adding CORAL to USHER
raises its mixing further (batch\_v2 0.7448) without changing this pattern.

\frost reaches this point of the frontier at a fraction of the cost. USHER recomputes exact structure matrices
200 times per pair, against 8 for \frost ($2BT$ against $2B$ with $T=25$). USHER's EM took 2{,}570--3{,}219\,s per
pair (mean 2{,}911\,s), with 54--67\% spent building structure matrices. \frost took 361.7--435.0\,s (mean
387.3\,s) at the same number of iterations, about 7.5-fold less. Both ran on the same hardware type, but the timing
difference also includes the other three differences between the pipelines.
