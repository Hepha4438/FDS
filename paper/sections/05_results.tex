\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{A spatially aware baseline fails in the reference-preserving setting}\label{sec:res_spatial}
On a common subsample of 10{,}000 cells per side (Table~\ref{tab:graphst}), \frost transfers labels more accurately
than GraphST (median paired difference $+0.376$, $n=12$, $p=0.0005$, the smallest value attainable with 12 pairs).
Mixing does not differ significantly ($+0.092$, $n=12$, $p=0.064$), so GraphST loses transfer without gaining mixing
in exchange. GraphST is also worse than no alignment on transfer (mean difference $-0.243$, $n=12$, $p=0.0015$); its only
gain over it is mixing (mean $+0.377$, $p=0.0005$), the signature of over-correction. \frost improves on no alignment in both
transfer ($p=0.0010$) and mixing ($p=0.0005$).

GraphST also moves the reference: the 15-NN neighbourhoods of reference cells barely survive integration (drift
0.966--0.989, mean 0.979), so anything built on the reference must be rebuilt. For \frost and every other
reference-preserving method drift is exactly zero, by architecture rather than tuning. Finally, GraphST had to be
subsampled because its dense spatial graph exhausted memory at 90{,}000 cells, whereas \frost runs on all
40{,}000--45{,}000 cells per side (GraphST wall-clock on the subsample: \todo{from log 42}).

\subsection{Benchmark}\label{sec:res_bench}
Because batch\_v2 and bio\_v2 average other metrics, ranking all 15 cell-level metrics would count the same information
several times; we summarise with the compact set of Table~\ref{tab:main} (all 15 in Supplementary
Figure~\ref{fig:grid}). On all seven cell-level metrics \frost ranks among the top three of 11 methods: first on
xfer\_all; second on batch\_v2, total\_v2, rare\_purity and knn\_xen; third on bio\_v2 and xfer\_main. The leader is
usually one of its own ablations; the one baseline that leads a column is ridge on bio\_v2. The imputation column
favours \frost only under a protocol that leaks the predicted gene, and the ranking reverses once the leak is removed
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). Figure~\ref{fig:umap} shows the hardest pair, COAD Stereo-seq.

Ridge is the informative comparison (Figure~\ref{fig:gaps}): it uses the same auxiliary labels and moment matching as
\frost and differs mainly in how cells are paired. \frost exceeds ridge on batch\_v2 ($n=12$, $p=0.0005$), total\_v2
($n=12$, $p=0.0005$) and knn\_xen ($n=12$, $p=0.0015$), and does not differ significantly on bio\_v2 (median
$-0.006$, $n=12$, $p=0.06$; ridge slightly higher), xfer\_main ($n=12$, $p=0.29$), xfer\_all ($n=11$, $p=0.64$) or
rare\_purity ($n=12$, $p=0.68$). total\_v2 is not independent evidence: batch\_v2 spans 0.34--0.66 across methods
and bio\_v2 only 0.60--0.69, so total\_v2 follows mixing. The knn\_xen advantage is specific to ridge; against
centring and Harmony it is not significant. Read as a Pareto trade-off, \frost gains mixing over ridge with no
significant loss in conservation or transfer. \frost exceeds Symphony, the published reference-mapping method, on all
seven metrics ($n=12$, $p$ from 0.0005 to 0.034), although part of that gap may reflect Symphony's own count-based
representation. Against centring and Harmony, \frost is significantly better on most metrics but not on xfer\_main
versus centring or knn\_xen versus either (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:center_harmony}).

\subsection{Ablations and cost}\label{sec:res_ablation}
\paragraph{Post-hoc moment matching.}\label{sec:res_coral}
Comparing USHER-CC with USHER-ST isolates CORAL. It raises batch\_v2 ($+0.127$, $p=0.0010$), total\_v2 ($+0.053$,
$p=0.0010$), rare\_purity ($+0.029$, $p=0.0015$) and xfer\_all ($+0.025$, $p=0.012$), with no significant change
in bio\_v2 ($p=0.23$), xfer\_main ($p=0.73$) or knn\_xen ($p=0.15$; $n=12$ throughout). On the GraphST subsample it
raises mean mixing by $+0.191$ ($p=0.0015$) and mean transfer by only $+0.009$. CORAL is therefore justified at the cell level;
its cost appears at the gene level (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). The per-class reference floor was fixed in
advance on a validation split (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:floor}).

\paragraph{Structure matrices and microenvironment.}\label{sec:res_niche}
\frost and USHER-CC do not differ significantly on any metric (all $p\ge0.067$, largest median difference 0.0008).
Because the difference bundles several changes to $\Cs$, the contribution of the microenvironment itself is not
identified (Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:supp_niche}). What the cached construction changes is cost, which is the
reason we propose it (Table~\ref{tab:cost}). Comparing the two ways of building structure at the same 25 EM
iterations on the four COAD pairs, caching $\Cs$ reduces structure builds from 104 to 8 (13-fold) and EM wall-clock
from 457.7 to 386.1\,s per pair (about 15\%). Adding the microenvironment block to the cached construction changes
neither (8 builds, 387.3\,s). The saving grows with the number of iterations, $B(T+1)$ against $2B$: 13-fold at
$T=25$ and 5.5-fold at the $T=10$ used in the benchmark. The cached configuration without the microenvironment was
timed but not scored, so the claim for the block is cost-neutrality, not accuracy.

\subsection{Comparison with USHER}\label{sec:res_usher}
On the four COAD pairs with a single USHER seed, no paired test can reach significance (exact floor $p=0.125$), so we
report differences descriptively (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:usher}); the pipelines also differ in four components
at once. Total scores are similar (0.7055 for USHER, 0.7025 for \frost). USHER is numerically higher on batch mixing;
\frost is numerically higher on biological conservation and on every task metric, most on xfer\_all, rare\_purity and knn\_xen
($+0.034$ to $+0.038$). \frost reaches this point of the frontier with 8 instead of 200 structure computations per pair
and about 7.5-fold less EM time (mean 387.3\,s against 2{,}911\,s).
