\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{A spatially aware baseline fails in the reference-preserving setting}\label{sec:res_spatial}
On a common subsample of 10{,}000 cells per side (Table~\ref{tab:graphst}), \frost transfers labels more accurately
than GraphST (median paired difference $+0.377$, $n=12$, $p=0.0005$; GraphST averaged over three seeds).
Mixing does not differ significantly ($+0.092$, $n=12$, $p=0.064$), so GraphST loses transfer without gaining mixing
in exchange. GraphST is also worse than no alignment on transfer (mean difference $-0.243$, $n=12$, $p=0.0015$); its only
gain over it is mixing (mean $+0.377$, $p=0.0005$), the signature of over-correction. \frost improves on no alignment in both
transfer ($p=0.0010$) and mixing ($p=0.0005$).

The smallest \frost$-$GraphST gap in label transfer across the twelve pairs (0.211) exceeds the largest across-seed
standard deviation observed for GraphST (0.019) by more than an order of magnitude; seed choice does not account for
the gap (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:graphst_seeds}).
GraphST also moves the reference (drift 0.965--0.989 across pairs and seeds, mean 0.979), so anything built on it must be rebuilt. GraphST had to be subsampled (its dense spatial
graph exhausted memory at 90{,}000 cells), whereas \frost runs on all 40{,}000--45{,}000 cells per side. GraphST
training took 883--1{,}152\,s per run on the subsample (mean 1{,}067\,s over 36 runs), and the \frost EM loop 387.3\,s
on all cells of the four COAD pairs at $T=25$ ($T=10$, used in the benchmark, was not timed), both excluding input preparation.

\subsection{Benchmark}\label{sec:res_bench}
Because batch\_v2 and bio\_v2 average other metrics, we summarise with the compact set of Table~\ref{tab:main}
(all 15 metrics in Supplementary Figure~\ref{S-fig:grid}). On all seven cell-level metrics \frost ranks among the top three of 11 methods, counting its two internal variants (Supplementary
Table~\ref{S-tab:main_variants}): first on
xfer\_all; second on batch\_v2, total\_v2, rare\_purity and knn\_xen; third on bio\_v2 and xfer\_main. The only column led by an external baseline is bio\_v2, led by ridge, which uses the same
reference-derived labels as \frost; the difference is not significant ($p=0.06$). The imputation column
favours \frost only under a protocol that leaks the predicted gene, and the ranking reverses once the leak is removed
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). Figure~\ref{fig:umap} shows the hardest pair, COAD Stereo-seq. Without alignment, and after Harmony, the two
platforms occupy separate regions; USHER-ST brings them into contact but leaves them partly segregated; after
\frost the platforms interleave throughout while the major cell types remain in distinct groups.

Ridge is the informative comparison (Supplementary Figure~\ref{S-fig:gaps}): it uses the same auxiliary labels and moment matching as
\frost and differs mainly in how cells are paired. \frost exceeds ridge on batch\_v2 ($n=12$, $p=0.0005$), total\_v2
($n=12$, $p=0.0005$) and knn\_xen ($n=12$, $p=0.0015$), and does not differ significantly on bio\_v2 (median
$-0.006$, $n=12$, $p=0.06$; ridge slightly higher), xfer\_main ($n=12$, $p=0.29$), xfer\_all ($n=11$, $p=0.64$) or
rare\_purity ($n=12$, $p=0.68$). total\_v2 is not independent evidence: batch\_v2 spans 0.34--0.66 across methods
and bio\_v2 only 0.60--0.69, so total\_v2 follows mixing. The knn\_xen advantage is specific to ridge; against
centring and Harmony it is not significant. Read as a Pareto trade-off (Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}), \frost gains mixing over ridge
with no significant loss in conservation or transfer. Stratifying by representation does not change this reading:
within the scGPT-spatial pairs ($n=7$) \frost exceeds ridge on batch\_v2, total\_v2, knn\_xen and imputation ($p\le0.031$)
with no significant difference on the other metrics, and within the PCA pairs ($n=5$) the same four differences have
the same sign at the smallest attainable $p=0.0625$ (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:strat}). \frost exceeds Symphony, the published reference-mapping method, on all
seven metrics ($n=12$, $p$ from 0.0005 to 0.034); against centring and Harmony it is better on most metrics (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:center_harmony}), and
against Harmony, fitted transductively on the evaluation tiles, these margins are lower bounds (Section~\ref{sec:reps}).

\subsection{Comparison with USHER}\label{sec:res_usher}
On the four COAD pairs, with one USHER seed, no paired test can reach significance (floor $p=0.125$), so we report
differences descriptively (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:usher}); the pipelines also differ in four components, and
\frost runs 10 iterations against USHER's 25, a bias against \frost. Total scores are similar (0.7055 for USHER, 0.7025
for \frost); USHER is numerically higher on batch mixing, \frost on conservation and on every task metric, most on
xfer\_all, rare\_purity and knn\_xen ($+0.034$ to $+0.038$), with 8 instead of 200 structure computations per pair and
about 7.5-fold less EM time (387.3 against 2{,}911\,s).

\section{Ablation studies}\label{sec:res_ablation}
\paragraph{Post-hoc moment matching.}\label{sec:res_coral}
Comparing USHER-CC with USHER-ST isolates CORAL. It raises batch\_v2 ($+0.127$, $p=0.0010$), total\_v2 ($+0.053$,
$p=0.0010$), rare\_purity ($+0.029$, $p=0.0015$) and xfer\_all ($+0.025$, $p=0.012$), with no significant change
in bio\_v2 ($p=0.23$), xfer\_main ($p=0.73$) or knn\_xen ($p=0.15$; $n=12$ throughout). On the GraphST subsample it
raises mean mixing by $+0.194$ ($p=0.0015$) and mean transfer by only $+0.008$. CORAL is therefore justified at the cell level;
its cost appears at the gene level (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). The per-class reference floor was introduced to protect rare types: on the four COAD pairs (three seeds) it raises
rare\_purity by 0.020, about nine times the seed standard deviation, while every other compact metric changes by at
most 0.006. The floor of 200 is kept in the reported configuration.

\paragraph{Identity regularisation and M-step.}
Raising $\lambda_{\mathrm{id}}$ above 0.3 lowers mixing and total score on both pairs; $\lambda_{\mathrm{id}}=0$ was
evaluated only together with a two-layer M-step (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:sens}). USHER's two-layer M-step
($\lambda_{\mathrm{id}}=0$, no target floor) mixes more, while the linear map transfers labels and predicts
microenvironment better; the two branches differ in architecture, $\lambda_{\mathrm{id}}$ and floor simultaneously,
so this is a pipeline-level comparison. We keep the linear map for the conservation end of the trade-off
(Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:sens}).

\paragraph{Building structure once: bundled evidence on 12 pairs.}\label{sec:res_once}
USHER-CC rebuilds $\Cs$ on $f_\theta(\mathbf{X}^{s})$ at every iteration; \frost builds it once on the unmapped source
and caches it. Across the 12 pairs the two do not differ significantly on any metric ($n=12$; batch\_v2 $p=0.52$,
bio\_v2 $p=0.95$, total\_v2 $p=0.43$, xfer\_main $p=0.85$, xfer\_all $p=0.26$, rare\_purity $p=0.72$, knn\_xen
$p=0.30$, imputation $p=0.23$; largest absolute median difference 0.0008). This is bundled evidence, not a
single-variable ablation: the two configurations also differ in the landmark approximation, the microenvironment
block and, on the PCA pairs, the 64-dimensional projection, so effects of opposite sign could cancel. A
comparison that isolates how $\Cs$ is built exists only on the four COAD pairs, below.

\paragraph{Structure matrices and microenvironment.}\label{sec:res_niche}
Three diagnostic arms on the four COAD pairs (seed 0, 25 iterations) take the bundled difference apart
(Table~\ref{tab:arms}); they differ only in how $\Cs$ is built: (a) landmark distances recomputed every iteration, as in USHER-ST; (b) exact
geodesics, cached; (c) \frost, which adds the microenvironment; the configuration differs from the main benchmark, so the
tables are not directly comparable. Going from (a) to (b)
changes two things---the landmark approximation and the per-iteration recomputation---and leaves quality unchanged
(total\_v2 $-0.005$, imputation $+0.001$), while convergence over the last three iterations becomes 5.65-fold deeper
(fraction of assignments changing: 0.046 to 0.008). Going from (b) to (c) adds only the microenvironment on the two
scGPT-spatial pairs; on the two PCA pairs it also changes the dimensionality projection. Stereo-seq is one of the
clean pairs, and it is where the effect appears: under the original imputation protocol, (c) raises Stereo-seq
imputation from 0.048 to 0.113 (bootstrap intervals 0.045--0.052 and 0.107--0.118), above no alignment (0.0945),
while convergence is unchanged on average (1.08-fold) but 2.44-fold slower on that pair. Under the leakage-controlled
protocol the gain persists in the benchmark configuration (\frost $-$ USHER-CC $=+0.044$ on COAD Stereo-seq,
disjoint intervals), but \frost stays below no alignment (0.1251 against 0.1850; Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
Averaged over the 12 pairs the term is neutral, so we do not claim that it improves the method. We keep it because it
is the only component that carries spatial information, it costs nothing, and it is the only one that recovers
gene-level signal on the source with the coarsest spatial sampling.

Stopping at 10 iterations costs a small, measurable amount: all three arms still change 6--8\% of assignments per iteration at iteration
10 (Supplementary Figure~\ref{S-fig:converge}), yet \frost scores total\_v2 0.7025 at 10 iterations (three seeds) and
0.706 at 25 (one seed) on the same pairs, against a per-pair seed standard deviation of about 0.002. Arm (a) differs
from the USHER implementation in the auxiliary cost, $\varepsilon$, the $\Cs$ approximation and the M-step, yet ends
close to where USHER stops (0.046 against 0.059, medians at the end), suggesting that recomputing $\Cs$ every
iteration, which they share, matters more for convergence than those four components.

Caching, not the landmark approximation, is also where the cost saving comes from. From arm (a) to arm (b), EM
wall-clock falls from 457.7\,s to 386.1\,s ($T=25$) although an exact build takes more thread time than a landmark
build (30.6 against 3.3\,s): the saving comes from building 4 rather than 100 source matrices. From (b) to (c), the
microenvironment block changes wall-clock by $+1.2$\,s, small against the spread across pairs (355--435\,s);
an EM iteration takes 15.4\,s with cached exact $\Cs$ and 18.3\,s with recomputed landmarks (Supplementary
Table~\ref{S-tab:cost}). Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:changes} maps every change from USHER to its ablation. Structure builds fall from $B(T+1)$ to $2B$, which no
longer depends on $T$: 5.5-fold fewer at $T=10$ and 13-fold fewer at $T=25$; wall-clock at $T=10$ was not measured.
