\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Integrating spatial platforms into a fixed reference is a different problem from integrating them jointly, and it
changes which methods work. A spatially aware joint method that is well suited to sections of one platform transfers
labels worse than no alignment here, and it rebuilds the reference it was asked to serve. \frost keeps the reference
fixed by construction and transfers labels at least as well as every baseline. It occupies a point on the
mixing--conservation frontier that favours cell-type structure and rare types over mixing, with 25-fold fewer structure computations and several-fold less run time than the algorithm it
builds on. The limits are as clear as the gains: alignment in embedding space does
not recover gene-level information, and the moment-matching step that gives \frost its mixing costs local density
fidelity.
