\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Gene-level tasks.}
Alignment does not help tasks that operate on genes, and we do not claim that it does. For imputation, the original
protocol predicted held-out genes of source cells from their reference neighbours. In that protocol the source
embedding is computed from all genes, including the one being predicted, so the leaked signal sits inside the
embedding that the map moves, and its effect differs between methods. We therefore reversed the protocol: query
reference cells and predict from mapped source neighbours. The leak then sits in the frozen reference embedding,
identical for all methods. Under the reversed protocol, standard baselines (no alignment, MNN, Harmony, Scanorama)
exceed every USHER-family variant, including \frost, in 11 of 12 pairs. On Stereo-seq the gap is large: 0.1850
for no alignment and 0.2089 for Scanorama against at most 0.1409 for the USHER family on COAD; 0.1248 for MNN
against 0.0882 on OV; and 0.2892 for no alignment against 0.1107 on HCC. Per-pair values for the nine
non-Stereo-seq pairs under this protocol were not archived. An earlier gene-masking protocol, run only on PCA embeddings and without \frost,
gave the same ordering for the variants it covered.

\paragraph{Cell-type deconvolution.}
We did not position \frost as a deconvolution method, and the boundary is architectural rather than empirical:
reference-based deconvolution consumes counts against a gene-by-cell-type signature matrix and rests on the
additivity of mRNA ($\mathbf{y}\approx\mathbf{S}\mathbf{w}$), whereas \frost emits embeddings with no gene axis,
no preserved expression scale and no additivity. We nonetheless asked the narrower question of whether alignment
helps a \emph{neighbour-based} proportion estimator, using pseudo-spots of $k=4$ and $k=8$ nearest cells. Their
median enclosing diameters are 49--65\um and 91--124\um, bracketing the 55\um Visium spot. We tested 11 technology
pairs at both spot sizes (22 settings). Alignment does not help. Simple centring is the only method never worse
than the prior-composition floor (mean JSD 0.0855 against 0.1242 for \frost, which wins 0 of 22 settings;
$p=0.001$). Post-hoc CORAL degrades 16 of 22 settings on both JSD and Pearson correlation. We therefore report only
the narrow claim, and note that count-based models such as RCTD and cell2location were not evaluated. The CORAL
result matters in its own right, because it quantifies a trade-off of our best-performing configuration: CORAL buys
$+0.191$ in batch mixing ($p=0.0015$) for $+0.0086$ in label transfer, at a measurable cost in local density
fidelity.

\paragraph{The microenvironment term.}
Its contribution cannot be separated from that of freezing exact structure matrices
(Section~\ref{sec:res_niche}). Its only measurable effect is confined to one pair. The microenvironment-only variant
was run with a single seed. The 15-neighbour radius ranges from 16.7\um (Visium HD FF) to 33.9\um (CosMx), so the
two structure matrices compare neighbourhoods at different physical scales. HCC Stereo-seq retains only 236--237
genes for imputation, too few for a conclusion.

\paragraph{Scope of the evaluation.}
SPATCH provides three patients per tissue; we used one, so the 12 pairs come from three patients and share a
reference within each tissue. Paired tests describe consistency across these pairs, not generalisation across
patients. The most informative extension would be the Stereo-seq pairs of the remaining patients. Only one spatially
aware baseline was run, at its published defaults, with one seed and on a subsample; STAligner could not be
installed. Harmony and Scanorama were fitted transductively on held-out tiles, which favours them. The USHER
reference implementation was run with one seed on four pairs. Timings come from diagnostic runs at 25 iterations;
peak memory was not measured.
