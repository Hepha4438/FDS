\section{Scope and limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Global alignment blurs gene-level neighbourhoods.}
\frost fits one global map so that source cells match the reference in distribution and label neighbourhoods; nothing
in that objective preserves each cell's gene-expression neighbourhood. Four measurements agree. Under
leakage-controlled imputation, archived for the three Stereo-seq pairs, no alignment exceeds \frost 1.5-, 1.6- and
4.9-fold (COAD, OV, HCC; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:imp_rev}). An earlier gene-masking protocol on PCA embeddings
ranked no alignment above centring above USHER. In neighbour-based deconvolution centring is best, and the
proportions from USHER-family maps are too diffuse (below). The CORAL trade-off is the same phenomenon at a smaller
scale. This does not hold for alignment in general. Local corrections keep gene-level neighbourhoods about as well as
no alignment (COAD Stereo-seq: MNN-ref 0.2015, Harmony 0.1792, raw 0.1850), but they mix far less: mean iLISI over
12 pairs is 0.0012 for raw, 0.024 for MNN-ref, 0.062 for Harmony and 0.486 for \frost. Global reference-preserving
maps sit at the high-mixing end of this frontier. That is why we do not position \frost as a gene-level method.

\paragraph{Why the original imputation protocol favoured \frost.}
It predicts each gene of a mapped source cell from its 15 reference neighbours. The source embedding is computed from
all genes, including the predicted one, so the map moves the very embedding that carries the leak; the protocol
rewards alignment and leakage together, and the two cannot be separated. This is why no alignment, despite leaking
the most, scores low there (COAD Stereo-seq: 0.0945 against 0.1041 for \frost): without alignment its reference
neighbours are meaningless. The reversed protocol queries reference cells, whose embedding is frozen and identical
across reference-preserving methods (not for Harmony, which modifies it), so the leak cancels. Two independent
leakage controls agree, but the reversed protocol has not been checked for artefacts of its own, and the decisive
test, gene masking by fold on scGPT-spatial embeddings, was not run. The cell-level results of
Section~\ref{sec:results} involve no gene prediction and are unaffected.

\paragraph{Deconvolution.}
Reference-based deconvolution relies on counts, a signature matrix and the additivity of mRNA, none of which an
embedding preserves, so \frost is not a deconvolution method by design. On the narrower question of whether alignment
helps a neighbour-based proportion estimator (pseudo-spots of 4 and 8 cells, 49--65\um and 91--124\um across, 22
settings), it does not: centring is best (mean JSD 0.0855 against 0.1242 for \frost, $p=0.001$), and CORAL degrades
most settings (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:deconv}). Count-based models such as RCTD~\citep{cable2022rctd} and
cell2location~\citep{kleshchevnikov2022cell2location} were not evaluated.

\paragraph{Scope of the evaluation.}
We used one of the three SPATCH patients per tissue, so paired tests describe consistency across pairs that share
references, not generalisation across patients. Only one spatially aware baseline was run, at defaults, with one
seed and on a subsample, and STAligner could not be installed. The USHER comparison rests on one seed and four pairs.
The microenvironment-only variant was run with one seed, and the 15-neighbour radius differs between platforms
(16.7--33.9\um).
