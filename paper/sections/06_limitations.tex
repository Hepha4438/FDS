\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Building structure once.}
The comparisons of Sections~\ref{sec:res_once} and~\ref{sec:res_niche} show that computing $\Cs$ once, from the
unmapped source, does not degrade the correspondences on these data; they do not cover a source whose geometry is
distorted enough that rebuilding $\Cs$ on the mapped image would help.

\paragraph{Reference labels in the auxiliary cost.}
\frost's auxiliary cost is seeded by a 15-NN vote of reference labels whose accuracy on held-out tiles is 0.69--0.79
across the four COAD pairs, i.e.\ 21--31\% of the transferred labels are wrong; on these pairs the method attains
the performance reported here under that level of label noise. This is the noise that arises naturally in our
pipeline on four COAD pairs; we ran no controlled label-corruption study and do not claim robustness to label noise
in general. The ridge baseline receives the same reference-derived labels.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to settings.}
The floor, $\lambda_{\mathrm{id}}$ and the M-step are varied in Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:sens}, the
construction of $\Cs$ in Table~\ref{tab:arms} and the number of EM iterations in Section~\ref{sec:res_niche};
$\alpha$, $\gamma$ and $k$ keep USHER's published values, whose sensitivity USHER reports (Figs.~S2--S4
of~\citep{pratapa2025usher}).

\paragraph{Choosing a configuration.}
For cell-level tasks, batch mixing and label transfer, \frost with moment matching is the configuration to use
(Section~\ref{sec:res_coral}). For gene-level tasks, or analyses that depend on local
expression geometry, we recommend leaving the source unaligned or applying a local correction: under the
leakage-controlled protocol no alignment exceeds \frost on all three Stereo-seq pairs
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). If a \frost map is needed anyway, CORAL should be omitted: \frost without CORAL
scores above \frost on reversed imputation on the three Stereo-seq pairs (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:imp_rev}) and
on neighbour-based deconvolution (mean JSD 0.1123 against 0.1242; Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:deconv}).

\paragraph{The microenvironment term.}
Its effect was observed only on Stereo-seq sources, and neither measure of spatial extent available to us, the
15th-neighbour distance or the diameter of 4-cell pseudo-spots, singles Stereo-seq out (Supplementary Section~S4), so
we have no basis for an automatic criterion. The
term is optional; we recommend deciding it by validation on a held-out part of the training tiles. Its neighbourhood
size $k$ (5, 15, 30) was varied on the four COAD pairs, which include the Stereo-seq pair where the effect exists,
scoring imputation only under the leakage-controlled protocol (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:kniche}): $k$ has no
effect on three pairs (range at most 0.005), while on Stereo-seq imputation rises with $k$, the intervals at $k=5$ and
$k=30$ are disjoint, and $k=30$ exceeds the reported $k=15$ by 0.0129 (the original protocol shows the same trend,
$+0.0127$ from $k=5$ to $k=30$ against $+0.0136$ here). Even at $k=30$ \frost stays below no alignment on that pair
(0.1386 against 0.1850): the sweep narrows the gene-level gap but does not close it. We keep $k=15$, fixed before the
sweep, because changing it now would select on the evaluation tiles and imputation lies outside the method's
recommended use. The label vote of the auxiliary cost has its own $k$, which was not varied. A fixed $k$ does not fix
the radius: at $k=15$ it is 16.7\um on Visium HD FF and 28.7\um on CosMx 6K in COAD, and across $k$ it spans
10.5--41.1\um; a radius-based neighbourhood is the alternative.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Global alignment blurs gene-level neighbourhoods.}
\frost fits one global map so that source cells match the reference in distribution and label neighbourhoods; nothing
in that objective preserves each cell's gene-expression neighbourhood. Four measurements agree. Under
leakage-controlled imputation, archived for the three Stereo-seq pairs, no alignment exceeds \frost 1.5-, 1.6- and
4.9-fold (COAD, OV, HCC; Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:imp_rev}), and gene masking agrees (below). In neighbour-based deconvolution centring is best, and the
proportions from USHER-family maps are too diffuse (below). The CORAL trade-off is the same phenomenon at a smaller
scale. This does not hold for alignment in general. Local corrections keep gene-level neighbourhoods about as well as
no alignment (COAD Stereo-seq: MNN-ref 0.2015, Harmony 0.1792, raw 0.1850), but they mix far less: mean iLISI over
12 pairs is 0.0012 for raw, 0.024 for MNN-ref, 0.062 for Harmony and 0.486 for \frost. Global reference-preserving
maps sit at the high-mixing end of this trade-off, which is why \frost is not positioned as a gene-level method.

\paragraph{Why the original imputation protocol favoured \frost.}
It predicts each gene of a mapped source cell from its reference neighbours, while the source embedding was computed
from all genes, including the predicted one; the protocol therefore rewards alignment and leakage together. That is
why no alignment, which leaks most, still scores low there (COAD Stereo-seq: 0.0945 against 0.1041 for \frost):
without alignment its reference neighbours are meaningless. Under this protocol \frost exceeds the label-matched
ridge ($p=0.0010$), CORAL ($p=0.0068$) and Scanorama ($p=0.034$), but does not differ significantly from centring
($p=0.30$), MNN-ref ($p=0.23$), Harmony ($p=0.23$) or no alignment ($p=0.052$; $n=12$ throughout): it separates
\frost from label-matched baselines, not from published methods. The reversed protocol queries frozen reference
cells, identical across reference-preserving methods (not for Harmony, which modifies them), so the leak cancels;
it agrees with an earlier gene-masking protocol that covered PCA embeddings and USHER but not \frost. The cell-level
results of Section~\ref{sec:results} involve no gene prediction and are unaffected.

\paragraph{Deconvolution.}
Reference-based deconvolution relies on counts, a signature matrix and the additivity of mRNA, none of which an
embedding preserves, so \frost is not a deconvolution method by design. On the narrower question of whether alignment
helps a neighbour-based proportion estimator (pseudo-spots of 4 and 8 cells, 49--65\um and 91--124\um across, 22
settings), it does not: centring is best (mean JSD 0.0855 against 0.1242 for \frost, $p=0.001$), and CORAL degrades
most settings (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:deconv}). The claim is specific to neighbour-based proportion
estimators; count-based models such as RCTD~\citep{cable2022rctd} and
cell2location~\citep{kleshchevnikov2022cell2location} rest on different assumptions.

\paragraph{Scope of the evaluation.}
The data comprise one patient per tissue, so paired tests describe consistency across pairs that share references,
not generalisation across patients. The comparison with spatial integration uses one method,
GraphST, on a subsample of 10{,}000 + 10{,}000 cells, at its published defaults with three seeds on all twelve
pairs and with two further neighbourhood sizes on the four COAD pairs. A second spatially aware method, STAligner,
could not be run in our environment, so the measured findings on transfer, mixing and run time concern GraphST only;
reference drift, which follows from re-embedding both sides, applies to joint integration methods as a class. The
per-class floor was varied on the COAD pairs only. Building one exact structure matrix scales quadratically with the
number of sketched cells (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:cost}), and run time was not measured beyond about 45{,}000
cells per side. Like USHER, \frost solves balanced FGW; unbalanced or partial variants are outside this
evaluation, and the per-class floor addresses mass imbalance between cell-type groups, not incomplete overlap between
sections. For the same reason, a cell type present in the source but absent from the reference must still be
transported and will be spread over the nearest reference types. The entropy and confidence filters inherited
unchanged from USHER (rows above the 90th entropy percentile or below the 10th confidence percentile of each batch are
left out of the M-step) keep such diffuse rows out of the fit of $f_\theta$, but those cells are still mapped, and
because the filters remove a fixed fraction of rows per batch, the fraction removed is not by itself a signal of
absent types. A diagnostic that should carry signal, which we have not tested, uses the value of the row entropy rather than the
fraction of rows removed: averaged over groups of source cells (source labels are not available in use, so clusters
of the mapped source), a type without a reference counterpart should show systematically higher entropy, and its
barycentric projections should lie farther from the nearest reference cell. Neither quantity is stored for the
reported configuration, so this requires a rerun with additional logging; the principled remedy, unbalanced or partial
transport, is likewise outside this evaluation. Label transfer is evaluated against the SPATCH reference
annotations as given; robustness to a noisier reference annotation lies outside the claims made here.
Directions that follow from these limits, among them unbalanced or partial transport, feature-level and
feature-weighted FGW, and a global map followed by a local correction, are set out in Supplementary Section~S7.
