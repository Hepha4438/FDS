\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Building structure once.}
Computing $\Cs$ from the unmapped source assumes that the source's own geometry, rather than its mapped image,
is what the correspondences need. The evidence for this comes at two strengths. On the four COAD pairs, arms (a) and (b)
differ only in how $\Cs$ is built (approximation and recomputation together); the change leaves total\_v2 and imputation unchanged and makes the assignments
converge further (Table~\ref{tab:arms}). On all 12 pairs, \frost and USHER-CC do not differ significantly on any
metric (largest absolute median difference 0.0008; Section~\ref{sec:res_once}), but that comparison bundles four
changes. Together they show that building structure once does not degrade the correspondences on these data; they do
not cover a source whose geometry is distorted enough that rebuilding $\Cs$ on the mapped image would help.

\paragraph{Reference labels in the auxiliary cost.}
\frost's auxiliary cost is seeded by a 15-NN vote of reference labels whose accuracy on held-out tiles is 0.69--0.79
across the four COAD pairs, i.e.\ 21--31\% of the transferred labels are wrong; the method attains its reported
performance under that level of label noise. The ridge baseline receives the same reference-derived labels. We did
not run a controlled label-corruption study.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to settings.}
The settings that \frost adds to USHER are varied in one table: the per-class floor, $\lambda_{\mathrm{id}}$ and the
M-step (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:sens}), with the construction of $\Cs$ in Table~\ref{tab:arms} and the
number of EM iterations in Section~\ref{sec:res_niche}. $\alpha$, $\gamma$ and $k$ keep the values published for
USHER, whose sensitivity to $\alpha$, $\gamma$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{fit}}$ is reported in~\citep{pratapa2025usher}
(Figs.~S2--S4 there). The comparison with ridge, the strongest external
baseline, reaches the same conclusions within the PCA pairs and within the scGPT-spatial pairs (Supplementary
Table~\ref{S-tab:strat}), so that comparison is not decided by the choice of representation.

\paragraph{Choosing a configuration.}
For cell-level tasks, batch mixing and label transfer, \frost with moment matching is the configuration to use:
adding CORAL to USHER-ST raises batch\_v2 ($+0.127$), total\_v2 ($+0.053$) and rare\_purity ($+0.029$) without a
significant change in bio\_v2 (Section~\ref{sec:res_coral}). For gene-level tasks, or analyses that depend on local
expression geometry, we recommend leaving the source unaligned or applying a local correction: under the
leakage-controlled protocol no alignment exceeds \frost on all three Stereo-seq pairs
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). If a \frost map is needed anyway, CORAL should be omitted: \frost without CORAL
scores above \frost on reversed imputation on the three Stereo-seq pairs (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:imp_rev}) and
on neighbour-based deconvolution (mean JSD 0.1123 against 0.1242; Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:deconv}).

\paragraph{The microenvironment term.}
Its effect was observed only on Stereo-seq sources; the other three technologies give no criterion that
generalises. The term is optional, and users should test it on their own data. The neighbourhood is a fixed number of cells whose
spatial extent differs between platforms (median 15th-neighbour distance 16.7--33.9\um; Supplementary Section~S4),
a further reason to treat the term as optional.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Global alignment blurs gene-level neighbourhoods.}
\frost fits one global map so that source cells match the reference in distribution and label neighbourhoods; nothing
in that objective preserves each cell's gene-expression neighbourhood. Four measurements agree. Under
leakage-controlled imputation, archived for the three Stereo-seq pairs, no alignment exceeds \frost 1.5-, 1.6- and
4.9-fold (COAD, OV, HCC; Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:imp_rev}), and gene masking agrees (below). In neighbour-based deconvolution centring is best, and the
proportions from USHER-family maps are too diffuse (below). The CORAL trade-off is the same phenomenon at a smaller
scale. This does not hold for alignment in general. Local corrections keep gene-level neighbourhoods about as well as
no alignment (COAD Stereo-seq: MNN-ref 0.2015, Harmony 0.1792, raw 0.1850), but they mix far less: mean iLISI over
12 pairs is 0.0012 for raw, 0.024 for MNN-ref, 0.062 for Harmony and 0.486 for \frost. Global reference-preserving
maps sit at the high-mixing end of this trade-off, which is why \frost is not positioned as a gene-level method.

\paragraph{Why the original imputation protocol favoured \frost.}
It predicts each gene of a mapped source cell from its reference neighbours, while the source embedding was computed
from all genes, including the predicted one; the protocol therefore rewards alignment and leakage together. That is
why no alignment, which leaks most, still scores low there (COAD Stereo-seq: 0.0945 against 0.1041 for \frost):
without alignment its reference neighbours are meaningless. Under this protocol \frost exceeds the label-matched
ridge ($p=0.0010$), CORAL ($p=0.0068$) and Scanorama ($p=0.034$), but does not differ significantly from centring
($p=0.30$), MNN-ref ($p=0.23$), Harmony ($p=0.23$) or no alignment ($p=0.052$; $n=12$ throughout): it separates
\frost from label-matched baselines, not from published methods. The reversed protocol queries frozen reference
cells, identical across reference-preserving methods (not for Harmony, which modifies them), so the leak cancels;
it agrees with an earlier gene-masking protocol that covered PCA embeddings and USHER but not \frost. The cell-level
results of Section~\ref{sec:results} involve no gene prediction and are unaffected.

\paragraph{Deconvolution.}
Reference-based deconvolution relies on counts, a signature matrix and the additivity of mRNA, none of which an
embedding preserves, so \frost is not a deconvolution method by design. On the narrower question of whether alignment
helps a neighbour-based proportion estimator (pseudo-spots of 4 and 8 cells, 49--65\um and 91--124\um across, 22
settings), it does not: centring is best (mean JSD 0.0855 against 0.1242 for \frost, $p=0.001$), and CORAL degrades
most settings (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:deconv}). The claim is specific to neighbour-based proportion
estimators; count-based models such as RCTD~\citep{cable2022rctd} and
cell2location~\citep{kleshchevnikov2022cell2location} rest on different assumptions.

\paragraph{Scope of the evaluation.}
The data comprise one patient per tissue, so paired tests describe consistency across pairs that share references,
not generalisation across patients. The comparison with spatial integration uses one representative method,
GraphST, at its published defaults and on a subsample of 10{,}000 + 10{,}000 cells, with three seeds on the four
COAD pairs and one seed on the others, so the claim that spatial integration fails in the reference-preserving setting
rests on that method. Like USHER, \frost solves balanced FGW; unbalanced or partial variants are outside this
evaluation, and the per-class floor addresses mass imbalance between cell-type groups, not incomplete overlap between
sections. Label transfer is evaluated against the SPATCH reference
annotations as given; robustness to a noisier reference annotation lies outside the claims made here.
