\begin{abstract}
Spatial transcriptomics platforms differ in gene panel, resolution and chemistry, so serial sections of one tissue
yield cell embeddings that do not agree across technologies. Most integration methods, including spatially aware
ones, re-embed all datasets jointly. This moves the reference and invalidates every annotation, classifier and index
built on it. We present \frost, a post-hoc method that maps cells from a source platform into a frozen reference
embedding with fused Gromov--Wasserstein transport. Building on USHER, \frost computes the within-domain structure
matrices once instead of in every EM iteration, fits a linear identity-regularised map, and finishes with
closed-form moment matching. We used the SPATCH data with Xenium 5K as the reference and four source platforms
(CosMx 6K, Visium HD FFPE, Visium HD FF and Stereo-seq) in three tumour types, giving 12 pairs. On seven summary
metrics, \frost ranks among the top three of 11 methods. It transfers labels more accurately than the spatially
aware GraphST in all 12 pairs (balanced accuracy 0.72 vs 0.37). GraphST is worse than no alignment in 11 pairs and
displaces its own reference almost entirely (neighbourhood drift 0.98, against zero for \frost by construction). On
four colon pairs, \frost and the USHER reference implementation reach the same total score from opposite sides of
the trade-off: \frost gives up some batch mixing for better conservation of cell-type structure and rare types. It
needs 25-fold fewer structure-matrix computations and about 7.5-fold less EM time. Alignment does not help
gene-level tasks: in imputation and neighbour-based deconvolution, standard baselines match or outperform \frost. We
report this as the boundary of embedding-level alignment.
\end{abstract}
